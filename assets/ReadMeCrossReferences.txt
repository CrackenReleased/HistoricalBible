WHAT AM I LOOKING AT?
Depicted in this chart are 63,779 cross-references found in the Bible.  Each cross reference is depicted by a single arc, the color of which corresponds to the distance between the two chapters, creating a rainbow-like effect. The bar chart running along the bottom represents all the chapters in the Bible, both Old and New Testaments, starting with Genesis 1 on the left (the first bar).  Books alternate in color between light and dark gray, with the first book of the Old and New Testaments in white.  The length of each bar denotes the number of verses in that chapter (for instance, the longest bar is the longest chapter in the Bible, Psalm 119).


WHAT IS A BIBLE CROSS-REFERENCE?
In some Bibles (and all study Bibles), you can find cross-references in the margin or bottom of the page.  Cross-references link concepts, locations, people, phrases, etc. found in different parts of the text. These are assembled by Biblical scholars and largely standardized in different versions of the Bible.  This particular chart uses cross-references from the King James Bible. 


WHO CREATED THIS GRAPHIC?
Dr. Chris Harrison is a Professor of Human-Computer Interaction at Carnegie Mellon University, with an interest in information visualization. He created the poster in collaboration with Pastor Christoph Römhild, who assembled a cross-reference dataset using a digital copy of the Bible. 


BACKGROUND
This set of visualizations started as a collaboration between Pastor Christoph Römhild and myself in 2007. He had assembled a digital dataset of cross references found in the King James Bible. Cross references are conceptual links between verses, connecting locations, people, phrases, etc. found in different parts of the Bible. Cross-references are included in the margins or footnotes of some Bibles. The pastor and I struggled to find an elegant solution to render the data – 63,779 cross references in total. We set our sights on something more beautiful than functional. At the same time, we wanted a visualization that honored and revealed the complexity of the Bible at every level – as one leans in, smaller details should become visible. This ultimately led us to the multi-colored arc diagram you see below. Jordan Peterson has included this graphic in his lecture series discussing how the Bible can be thought of as "the first hyperlinked book". https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f-wWBGo6a2w&t=4280s
